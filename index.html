How to breathe while lifting

One rule covers almost every exercise in this plan: exhale on the effort, inhale on the way back. The demos show the timing on every rep.

The rep rhythm

  1. Inhale through your nose during the lowering or returning part. Take 2–3 seconds.
  2. Short pause at the stretch. Stay tight.
  3. Exhale through pursed lips while you push or pull. About 1–2 seconds.

Bracing for heavy sets

On squats, RDLs, step-ups and presses in the Strength phase:

  1. Take a big breath into your belly, not your chest.
  2. Tighten your abs as if someone is about to poke your stomach.
  3. Lower the weight holding that brace, then exhale through the hardest part of the lift.

Never hold your breath for more than one rep. If you have high blood pressure, skip bracing and breathe on every rep.

Planks and core holds

Breathe slowly: 3 seconds in, 3 seconds out, through the nose. Keep the abs tight while the belly moves. If you feel you must hold your breath to keep the position, the hold is too long; end the set.

Between sets: 3 slow breaths with a long exhale bring your heart rate down faster.

How to progress

Use double progression. Each exercise has a rep range. When you hit the top of the range on every set with clean form, go up 5 lb (dumbbells) or one plate (stack) next session.

When 50 lb is easy

The rack tops out at 50 lb. Keep progressing by slowing the lowering to 4 seconds, adding a 2-second pause at the bottom, doing 1½ reps, or adding the weighted vest from the gear list.

Check-ins

  • Weigh every morning after the bathroom; use the weekly average
  • Waist at the belly button every 2 weeks
  • Front and side photos once a month, same light
  • Log the weight and reps of every set

Adjust every 2 weeks

  • Losing 0.5–1 lb a week: keep everything the same.
  • Losing more than 1.5 lb a week, or lifts dropping: add 150 kcal (1 roti or ½ cup rice).
  • No change for 3 weeks and waist not shrinking: remove 150 kcal or add 2,000 steps a day.
  • Around 163 lb with a smaller waist: move up to about 2,450 kcal to keep building.

Supplements worth it

  • Creatine monohydrate, 5 g every day, any time. The best-studied muscle supplement.
  • Whey protein only when food falls short of 150 g.
  • Check with a doctor first if you have kidney problems or take regular medication.

Recovery is part of the plan

  • Sleep 7–9 hours. Muscle is built while you sleep.
  • 8,000 steps a day, rising to 10,000 in the Define phase.
  • One planned treat meal a week is fine; 80% consistency wins.

Add-ons: sardines and protein bread

Wild-caught sardines · 3 lunches a week

Season skinless, boneless sardines in olive oil, 6 × 4.375 oz, about $13.99 ($2.33 a can). One can: about 22 g protein, 200 kcal, plus omega-3 fats that chicken doesn't have.

  • Swap: on 6 of the 15 days, replace the 150 g lunch chicken with 1 can of sardines + 1 extra egg.
  • That day: about 141 g protein and 2,300 kcal, still on target.
  • Make it as sardine masala with rice, or mash it with onion, lemon and chili.
  • Costs about $1.50 more per swap day than chicken. Buy 8 lb of chicken instead of 10.

Protein bread · 10 breakfasts

Rudi's Daily Power Protein bread, 2 × 18 oz loaves, about $9.99. Two slices: 10 g protein, 190 kcal, 7 g fiber. Only some Costco stores carry it.

  • Swap: replace the 50 g oats with 2 slices of toast. Calories stay the same and protein goes up 3.5 g.
  • Make an egg sandwich or masala omelette on toast. Keep oats on the other 5 days.
  • Freeze the second loaf on shopping day and toast slices straight from frozen.
  • Costs about $0.84 more per day than oats.
  • If your store doesn't carry it, look for a bread with at least 5 g protein and 3 g fiber per slice.

With both add-ons, food costs about $8.30 a day (about $125 for 15 days) and protein stays near 145–150 g on most days.

Prep routine

Shopping day

  • Split the chicken into 5 bags of 900 g (3 days × 300 g). One bag goes in the fridge; four go in the freezer.
  • Peel and slice the ripest bananas and freeze them for oats.
  • Put the walnuts in the freezer.

Every 3 days (days 1, 4, 7, 10, 13)

  • Cook the 900 g of chicken: half in the lunch style, half in the dinner style.
  • Cook 225 g dry rice, roast 450 g sweet potato, make chana masala from 1½ cans.
  • Portion into 3 lunch and 3 dinner boxes. Cooked chicken keeps 3–4 days in the fridge.
  • The night before the next cook day, move the next chicken bag from the freezer to the fridge.
